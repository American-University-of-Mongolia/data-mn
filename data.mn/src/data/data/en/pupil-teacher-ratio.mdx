methodology: |
  Pupils per teacher is the average number of pupils for each teacher at the selected education level. Primary and all-level ratios use their respective pupil and teacher populations. This measure is not class size and does not by itself measure teaching quality.

  The source is the Ministry of Education, published by NSO. NSO’s definition describes pupils per teacher, although its web metadata displays “%”. Values are retained unchanged and shown as pupils per teacher, not percentages.

  Years retain the annual labels published by NSO. The definition refers to a school year. For context, the Ministry’s 2025–2026 totals of 819,907 pupils and 39,126 teachers give 21.0 pupils per teacher after rounding, matching NSO’s 2025 all-level national value. Earlier source years have not been independently mapped to school-year spans.

  NSO cites its education-statistics methodology dated 16 December 2013. The table does not establish that definitions and coverage are unchanged throughout 2007–2025. Historical values are displayed as published, with no interpolation or adjustment.

  Downloads contain 874 observations: 19 years, two education levels and 23 locations (21 aimags, Ulaanbaatar and the national average). The overlapping macroregion aggregates are excluded; the city-level Ulaanbaatar entry is retained once. The national average is the official source value, not an average of aimag ratios.
